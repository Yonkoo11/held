# Art Direction — Held

Stage 1. The signature element is decided here, in words, before a single path is drawn.

## The lazy ideas, named and forbidden

Every product has an obvious first mark. For this one there are seven, and all seven are banned
for the rest of this pipeline. Naming them is the point: an unnamed cliché is one the drawing
drifts into.

| the lazy idea | why it is forbidden |
|---|---|
| **A padlock** | "Held" reads as "locked" to anyone not paying attention. It is also factually wrong: nothing here is secured against anyone, it is paused on its way somewhere. Also the single most exhausted mark in this category. |
| **A shield** | Same misreading, plus it claims a security posture the product does not make. |
| **A pause glyph** (two vertical bars) | The second-laziest route from the word "held". It is a media-player control. It owns nothing, and it says stopped without saying *going*. |
| **An hourglass or clock face** | There is a deadline in the product, so a clock is the obvious grab. Every escrow, every timer, every vesting product already uses it. Category wallpaper. |
| **A chain link, hexagon-node, or cube** | The crypto uniform. Explicitly on the universal forbidden list. |
| **A handshake, or two arrows meeting** | The escrow-industry cliché. Reads as "deal", which is not what happens here. |
| **An H monogram** | A monogram is the surrender: it encodes the spelling of the name and nothing about the product. |

Also inherited from the universal guardrails: no text, letters or numerals inside the mark; no
rainbow or second hue; no sparkles, glows or lens flares; no gradient blobs; no busy composition.

## The signature element

**A rail with a single solid block arrested on it, past the midpoint.**

Physically:

- One horizontal rail, drawn edge to edge, in ink. This is the path from the buyer to the seller.
- One solid block, in the accent, sitting centred **through** the rail rather than beside it, so
  the rail visibly passes behind and continues out the other side. The payment is on the path,
  not parked next to it.
- The block sits at roughly 59% of the rail's length. This asymmetry is the entire concept and
  is not negotiable: more rail behind it than in front of it means it has travelled, it is not
  at the start, and the short remaining run reads as the direction it will go if nobody
  intervenes. A centred block would say "balanced, waiting, could go either way", which is the
  behaviour of a card authorisation and the opposite of this product.

It is a transformation, not an icon: origin, arrest, remaining distance, read left to right in
one object. That is what the guardrails ask for over a static symbol.

## Why this is ownable

The page already draws this object at full scale as the wire diagram: `you ——[pod]—— the seller`.
The mark is the same object cropped and compressed, so the logo in the tab and the diagram in the
first viewport are one recurring symbol at two scales rather than two unrelated pieces of art.
That is the thing a competitor cannot lift without lifting the whole product idea.

## Material language

Flat, matte, no material trickery. Derived from the product's own world, which is a financial
document on warm paper: hairline rules, one ink, one accent, no depth effects. Glass, bevels,
inner glows and 3D would lie about a page that deliberately ships zero ambient glows and zero
texture overlays.

## Composition

Centred in a square field with generous margin. The rail runs the full width so the crop reads as
a close-up of a longer line rather than a floating widget. High-contrast silhouette: at any size
the shape is one horizontal stroke interrupted by one solid mass.

## Palette

| role | value |
|---|---|
| rail | ink `#17150f` |
| block | accent `#b0431c` |
| ground | paper `#faf8f4`, or transparent |

One accent hue. Everything else is the neutral the product already uses.

## Dual-legibility target

- **16px favicon:** the rail and the block survive as two shapes. Nothing else is present to lose.
- **512px and above:** the proportion between the travelled run and the remaining run becomes
  readable as a deliberate ratio, and the rail's pass-through behind the block is visible.

## Route

**Vector.** The mark is geometric, the floor is 16px, and the product's design language is flat
and matte. Authored as SVG directly and rendered natively at every target size. Nothing upscaled.
